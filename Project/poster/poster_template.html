Explainable Predictive Process Monitoring

Does it matter where an activity happens? Apriori vs. IMPresseD itemsets for location importance
Group 11  ·  Jerrik Faber, Max Hoijng, Job Oosterkamp, Bora Şenel, Floris Vermeulen

1Motivation and Scientific Background

Predictions need explanations

  • Outcome-oriented predictive process monitoring predicts how a running case ends, so an owner can act in time [2].
  • Black-box models (e.g. XGBoost) predict well but not why [2]; post-hoc explainable AI (XAI) explains a trained model [1].
  • Existing methods say which activities matter, not where in the trace. Location permutation importance [4] moves a set of activities (an itemset) to other positions: if the model gets worse, their location matters.
Our question: do other activities and locations come out as important if IMPresseD [5] chooses the itemsets instead of Apriori?

Input and output

  • In: an event log with, per case, the activities in execution order and a 0/1 outcome; control-flow only. BPIC11 has dates only, so events are ordered by event number.
  • B also needs case attributes (BPIC11: age, diagnosis and three codes) for its case distance.
  • Out: per itemset 50 F1 drops (5 folds × 10 repeats), shown as mean and spread.

Step 2: two ways to choose the itemsets

A · Apriori (original method)

  • Every single activity + the 10 most frequent itemsets of 2–3 activities (support ≥ 0.5).
Selects on: frequency; ignores the labels.

B · IMPresseD (our variant)

  • Patterns: activities linked directly (a → b) or eventually (a ⇒ b), scored on three interests:
Outcome ↑information gain on the label from the pattern count
Frequency ↑share of cases with the pattern
Case distance ↓cases with vs. without it (case attributes)
  • Keep the Pareto front, extend it by one activity, repeat up to length 3. Then drop the ordering: a → b, b → a, a ⇒ b all become {a, b}.
Selects on: what the pattern count says about the outcome (80 % training split).

4Limitations and Future Work

Limitations

  • Selection ≠ importance. Both strategies select on existence (frequency, pattern count); the importance step tests location. A pair whose location matters but whose presence does not is never tested (E2).
  • An itemset inherits the importance of its strongest activity (f2: every top itemset of B contains 376400).
  • B selects on 80 % of the cases, but the folds use all cases. One model, one hospital, date-only timestamps, a tiny synthetic log.

Future work

  • Location-aware selection: an IMPresseD interest on the position of a pattern instead of its count.
  • Report the gain of an itemset over its best subset; repeat the selection inside each fold.
  • Other models and encodings (e.g. LSTM, position from the end) and domains; permute a pattern as a whole, with its order.
